## Preface

First, I apologize for possibly taking up your valuable time, sacrificing your reading experience, and affecting your good mood. If you don't like this article, please feel free to ignore it. One thing you need not worry about, though: the Tropic Line staff each have their own duties, so writing this article will not affect the magazine's production schedule. The recent delay is mainly because many staff members had things to attend to during the Spring Festival, so progress is a bit slower.

I understand that in the vast field of creation, converging inspiration and similar ideas are natural occurrences. However, we found that the degree of similarity between an official column and our fan work seems to go **a little bit** beyond the scope of ordinary coincidences of inspiration.

We invest time, energy, and even heart and soul simply to express our love for the original work and the joy of sharing with fellow fans. Yet these efforts often go unrewarded, and may even be improperly borrowed from or used commercially. Although we have donation income and Bilibili incentives, and Suiwanzi once tipped about a hundred yuan too, this is far from enough to cover the costs of attending events and giving out copies for free. Behind every event, each staff member pays thousands out of pocket for printing, let alone being able to pay for the years of selfless dedication of even a single staff member. We have always created out of love, and this is entirely a money-losing endeavor, and in the end we still have to accept improper borrowing by the official side. This should not be, and ought not to be, the fate of a non-profit group.

There are some perhaps-not-coincidental coincidences: the question setup and section arrangement of the Islander Tea Chat (for example, in the Yang Yan interview, the questions are divided into “About Arknights,” “About Creation,” and “About Life”) share many similarities with the Tropic Line special interview section, which has been in use since August 2022.

Especially in the first two installments, the interviews with Yang Yan and Zc (in the latest installment most questions are marked as coming from netizens, so we set it aside for now), some questions not stated to be from netizens are almost word for word identical to Tropic Line's.

I fully understand that people doing this kind of interview can sometimes have very similar approaches, but similar approaches rarely produce identical questions and layouts. I also fully understand (and welcome) learning from predecessors' experience when writing, but could you perhaps let the original author know before learning from them?

This article only compares the **Tropic Line special interviews** (earliest August 2022; fan work) with the **Skland Islander Tea Chat** (earliest October 2023; official), to help readers better understand the similarities and differences between the two.
